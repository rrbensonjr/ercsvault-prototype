ERCESVault

In-Building Radio Coverage Managed in One Record

ERCESVault maintains a single record for each ERCES throughout its full lifecycle — from benchmark testing and frequency authorization through design, installation, acceptance, maintenance, and renewal.

One system. One project record. Clear responsibilities. A complete audit trail.

Each stakeholder manages the part of the process it owns:

  • Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ): receives complete, standardized submissions and retains the compliance record over the life of the system.
  • License/Frequency Holder (LFH): knows what systems are operating on its network and controls authorization to retransmit its licensed frequencies.
  • ERCES Integrator: designs and delivers systems against clearly defined, repeatable requirements, reducing rework and inconsistency across jurisdictions.

Three parties work in the record

Sets the standard every building must meet

Authority Having Jurisdiction

One set of requirements for every project in the jurisdiction: the adopted code, local amendments, and the critical areas that must always have coverage. Incomplete submittals are flagged before they reach a reviewer, and every decision stays with the AHJ.

  • Consistent requirements on every project
  • Submittals checked for completeness before review
  • Approvals, test results and renewals kept with the project record
  • Program requirements drawn directly from the jurisdiction's published documents
  • Works alongside your permitting system, not in place of it

Controls the radio network

License Frequency Holder

Written retransmission authorization, the technical criteria every system must meet, and a registry of every booster rebroadcasting the network's channels. ERCESVault doesn't grant spectrum authority. It gives the License Frequency Holder a better way to exercise it.

  • Retransmission authorization, conditions, expiration and renewal
  • Network-protection criteria every system must meet
  • A registry of installed boosters and the parties responsible for each
  • Systems near an interference event identified quickly
  • Buildings affected by a network change identified in advance
  • Access to technical information controlled by role

One Structured Life Cycle

Four stages connect code compliance, spectrum authorization, technical evidence and the ongoing record — and each stage has a party that owns it.

  1. Publish the ProgramThe code the jurisdiction adopted, its local amendments, and the criteria the radio system requires.The Authority Having Jurisdiction
  2. Manage the ProjectBenchmark, determination, design, installation verification and acceptance.The ERCES Integrator, reviewed by the Authority
  3. Authorize the SystemThe request, the technical review, the conditions, and the written retransmission authorization.The License Frequency Holder
  4. Manage the LifecycleThe registry, monitoring, maintenance, annual compliance, and modification or renewal.The ERCES Integrator, visible to both authorities
